Two access caches no longer grow with uptime: the at-ref models and the per-workspace memo of own access entries. (The per-workspace access model itself is unchanged: one held per workspace that was asked, and re-read when it is asked again more than five seconds later.) A cached at-ref model reads its commit's listing from bytes, one string per path, so it keeps no view onto the listing; commits that share most of their paths share one base set of them (a new commit is built against the closest of the eight most recently held bases, so up to eight diverging branches built in turn each keep their own), and such a commit's tree index costs what it changed. Measured on a 20,000-path tree: about 13 KB held per cached commit (before: the 1.7 MB listing and a 20,000-entry set). The per-workspace memo of a file's own access entries keeps only `.md`, `.tool` and other access-frontmatter files, copies what it keeps into strings of its own, drops a workspace's memo once it is five minutes old at the next batch check or invalidation on any workspace (that workspace need not be asked again), and holds at most 32 workspaces and 200,000 entries, oldest dropped first. These bounds, like the at-ref models' tree budget, are per service graph: a host that runs one graph per tenant holds one memo per active tenant, each within them. What the gates answer is unchanged.
